Project SMaRTER: AI-Driven Tower Fingerprinting and Asset Condition Assessment

Industry AI, Predictive Engineering, and Digital Twin Platform | Amplitel and UTS Telecom Research Institute (TRI)

A hybrid AI framework for portfolio-scale structural fingerprinting and risk-based predictive maintenance of telecommunication towers. The framework combines physics-based structural degradation modelling with maintenance evidence extracted from free-text records using large-language-model natural language processing. Monte Carlo simulations quantify uncertainty in long-term deterioration and probability of failure, while satellite-image processing, semantic segmentation, and geospatial feature engineering model site-specific consequences within each tower fall zone. The resulting probability- and consequence-of-failure profiles are integrated into actionable risk tiers through machine learning and subject-matter-expert (SME) rules, supporting inspection and maintenance prioritisation.

Contribution

Project lead and primary researcher for hybrid AI framework design, machine-learning and NLP development, structural degradation and uncertainty modelling, satellite-image analytics, portfolio-scale validation, technical delivery, research coordination, and industry engagement.

Methods

LLM-based NLP for maintenance data, probabilistic structural degradation modelling, Monte Carlo simulation, time-varying health indices, AHP component-criticality weighting, satellite-image processing, computer vision, fall-zone and geospatial feature engineering, unsupervised clustering, SME-defined engineering rules, and probability-consequence risk modelling.

Outcome

Improving asset-management decision-making for Australia's critical infrastructure through defensible and explainable AI and machine learning. The framework combines traceable evidence, interpretable asset fingerprints, and uncertainty-aware risk modelling to support transparent inspection, maintenance, renewal, and investment prioritisation at portfolio scale.

Multispectral Computer Vision and Machine Learning for Material Characterisation

Imaging-Based Non-Destructive Testing, Multispectral Computer Vision, and Machine Learning

Developed a new imaging-based non-destructive testing method and custom multispectral imaging platform for material characterisation. The work combines computer vision, spectral signal processing, machine learning, and linear and nonlinear spectral unmixing to detect defects, estimate metal-loss depth, and characterise the relative phase composition of materials. Convolutional autoencoder (CAE) encoder-decoder networks perform nonlinear spectral unmixing by projecting spatial-spectral image cubes into a constrained latent abundance space. The encoder learns spatial and spectral patterns associated with different material phases, while the constrained decoder reconstructs each measured spectrum from estimated endmember signatures and abundance fractions. Spectral-angle loss and Adam optimisation guide model training, producing per-pixel maps of relative material composition. These predictions were quantitatively validated against X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurements using Rietveld refinement.

Contribution

Designed and integrated a mechatronic multispectral imaging platform combining programmable LED illumination, switching and control electronics, monochrome-camera integration, thermal excitation, and controlled imaging geometry. Developed the complete computer-vision and signal-processing pipeline for image acquisition, calibration, illumination correction, spectral-feature extraction, linear and nonlinear spectral unmixing, quantitative validation, and publication.

Methods

Multispectral signal preprocessing, spatial-spectral feature engineering, PCA dimensionality reduction, machine-learning regression, linear and nonlinear spectral unmixing, convolutional encoder-decoder networks, endmember extraction, abundance-map reconstruction, and XRD-Rietveld validation.

Reliability Analysis of Corroded and Fatigued Steel Elements

Experimentally Informed Stochastic Micromechanics and Finite Element Modelling | RMIT University

An experimentally informed reliability framework for assessing the combined effects of corrosion and cyclic fatigue in mild-steel elements. Uncertainty in corrosion condition, micromechanical properties, grain morphology, and fatigue response is represented through stochastic modelling and propagated through three-dimensional grain-scale Representative Volume Element finite element models. Periodic boundary conditions reproduce material-scale behaviour, while ABAQUS stress analysis, FE-safe fatigue simulation, and probabilistic limit-state evaluation estimate fatigue life and probability of failure under uncertain degradation and cyclic loading. Nanoindentation, Electron Backscatter Diffraction, and experimental fatigue testing provide the material evidence used to parameterise and validate the models.

Contribution

Developed the stochastic modelling and reliability-analysis workflow, interpreted nanoindentation and EBSD measurements, constructed and evaluated microscale RVE models, performed numerical and experimental comparisons, and contributed to engineering analysis and manuscript development.

Methods

Stochastic micromechanical modelling, 3D grain-scale RVEs, corrosion-dependent material-property distributions, periodic boundary conditions, ABAQUS stress analysis, FE-safe fatigue simulation, nanoindentation, EBSD grain and phase mapping, S-N fatigue testing to ASTM E466-15, probability-of-failure estimation, and experimental validation.

Augmented-Reality-Enabled Physics-Based Digital Twin

Structural Health Monitoring, Finite Element Analysis, and Mobile AR | RMIT University

A physics-based digital twin for near-real-time monitoring of a laboratory-scale steel truss. A simplified 2D finite element model uses measured strain data, inverse load identification, and a physics-informed neural network (PINN) to reconstruct loading and structural deformation. The results are transmitted through a cloud backend and visualised over the physical structure using mobile augmented reality.

Contribution

Developed and integrated the instrumented truss, 2D structural model, PINN-assisted analysis, cloud connectivity, and mobile AR visualisation.

Methods

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) using structural-equilibrium constraints, constitutive relations, boundary-condition enforcement, sensor-data loss functions, and automatic differentiation; simplified 2D finite element modelling; stiffness-matrix assembly; strain-sensitivity analysis; regularised inverse load identification; nodal displacement reconstruction; and Firebase-connected mobile AR application development.

Outcome

A cloud-connected digital twin that reconstructs loading and deformation from measured strain data and visualises the structural response alongside the physical asset.

Terahertz Non-Destructive Testing

Terahertz Time-Domain Spectroscopy | Terahertz Engineering Laboratory, University of Adelaide

Application of terahertz time-domain spectroscopy as a non-destructive evaluation method for estimating defect severity and coating-layer thickness in structural specimens. Experimental testing was conducted at the Terahertz Engineering Laboratory within the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at the University of Adelaide. The work exploits the penetration, coherence, and high temporal resolution of terahertz waves to characterise material interfaces and detect defects beneath non-metallic coatings.

Contribution

Developed and refined a terahertz imaging setup, specimen-testing workflow, and data-processing pipeline for measuring coating and corrosion layers.

Methods

Terahertz time-domain spectroscopy; wavelet-based signal denoising and baseline correction; interface-echo localisation for layer-thickness estimation; time-of-flight analysis; Gaussian mixture modelling for separating overlapping pulse components; spatial terahertz image reconstruction; and corrosion-phase identification and classification.

Outcome

Demonstrated the capability of terahertz imaging as a non-contact, non-destructive testing method for subsurface defect detection, material-interface characterisation, and layer-thickness measurement in coated structural specimens, supporting broader applications in structural inspection and condition assessment.

Embedded Personal Safety and Route Anomaly Detection System

Third-Year Mini Project | Embedded Systems, GPS/GSM Tracking, and Probabilistic Route-Deviation Detection

A child-friendly embedded mobile device enabling an authorised parent to call, locate, and live-track a child without providing access to a conventional smartphone. An ATmega2560 microcontroller integrates NEO-6M GPS positioning, SIM800L GSM voice and messaging, local storage, battery management, and an Android parent application. Its novel probability-matrix tracking algorithm learns frequently travelled routes and detects statistically unlikely deviations, reducing false alarms associated with fixed-boundary geofencing.

Contribution

Designed, fabricated, and tested the embedded hardware and enclosure; integrated the GPS, GSM, power-management, user-interface, and Android application components; and developed the probability-based route-learning and deviation-detection algorithm. Implemented authorised calling and messaging, live location requests, SOS alerts, remote microphone activation, low-battery warnings, and last-known-position recovery.

Methods

ATmega2560 embedded control, UART-connected NEO-6M GPS and SIM800L GSM modules, Li-ion charging and DC boost power management, five-second location sampling, GSM voice and SMS communication, EEPROM recovery, and Android application integration. GPS observations are quantised into an 11 m spatial grid, accumulated into a route-probability matrix, and evaluated against a likelihood threshold to identify anomalous travel paths after an initial route-learning period. Location processing and storage remain on the authorised parent device rather than a third-party cloud service.

Publication

2020 IEEE ISEC, 1-7
